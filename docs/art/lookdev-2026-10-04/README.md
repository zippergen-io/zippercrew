## What stays consistent

The three redesigned identities, coral/teal/plum role accents, left Implementer,
right Reviewer, foreground Human, separate workstations, rear coffee counter,
shared board and large window. The concepts show comparable activity poses for
art evaluation; they do not specify a new workflow or simultaneous runtime state.

Implementer has an auburn quiff, angular headphones, a cropped utility jacket and
forward energy. Reviewer has a silver wedge silhouette, dark spectacles, broad
teal shoulders, a tablet and a skeptical expression. Human has a large indigo
updo, copper triangular clip, plum overshirt and grounded, observant posture.

## Assessment

**A is my recommendation to develop next.** Clean material fields, selective
bevels and stronger lit/shadow separation retain warmth with much less visual
clutter. It provides the clearest departure from the painterly production room
while preserving an inviting everyday workspace.

**B is the strongest graphic alternative.** Harder value shapes and angular
furniture improve small-scale readability. It remains somewhat faceted in the
clothes/hair; a production art pass would need to decide whether to retain those
planes or simplify them into smoother geometry with hard cel-lighting bands.

**C has the strongest atmosphere.** Dark steel, walnut, glass and focused lighting
give it an urban identity. Its dark clothing and furnishings need more careful
separation at gameplay scale than A or B. The light strips should remain
architectural accents, rather than growing into a neon aesthetic.

These are flattened concept images. They establish targets, not verified sprite
atlases, animations or production lighting layers. After a direction is selected,
the next check should be one isolated character and station in the existing
renderer before replacing the complete asset set.

C used that composition and those character identities as references. No
external game artwork was used as a reference. Final generated PNGs are copied
unchanged into this folder. The production reference is an existing app screenshot.
